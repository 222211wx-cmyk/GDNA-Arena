---
name: Empire CLIPS rules
description: Owner-mandated publishing rules for the Reels-style clips tab
---

- Clips duration rule: **minimum 1:00 for both audio and video, NO maximum**; rejection text is “خاص دقيقة الفوق”. **Why:** the final owner specification on 2026-09-18 explicitly includes audio, films and very long media. **How to apply:** enforce the minimum on both client and server; never restore the short-audio exception or a 24h cap.
- Publishing has no production category, rights declaration, original-footage checkbox, or music/fingerprint ownership gate. **Why:** the owner explicitly removed these from Clips in the final simple specification on 2026-09-18; this supersedes earlier first-owner publishing instructions. **How to apply:** preserve historical rights/finance data and authentication/object ownership controls, but do not use rights records to block new clip publication. Other products' rights systems are not part of this change.
- Successful publishing produces a filename-derived name and server-generated unique HELX-###### code. **Why:** the owner wants “تعطيه اسم وكود” rather than additional classification steps. **How to apply:** persist and return the code; do not show a client-generated placeholder before server confirmation.
- View earnings continue to feed the PPO revenue pool. **Why:** simplifying publishing did not authorize removing finance history or view accounting.
- Finite Clips media must never repeat automatically; natural completion proceeds through the ordered feed, and its final item stops. Keep play/pause available and respect deliberate pauses. **Why:** the owner explicitly rejected automatic replay on 2026-09-19 for video, audio files, stories, and selfies, asking instead for continuation to the next publication. **How to apply:** do not restore looping for immersive playback; standalone links without an ordered feed stop at completion.
- View minting: identity from proxy-derived `req.ip` only (trust proxy 1), dedupe + earnings update in one transaction — raw X-Forwarded-For was review-rejected as forgeable.
- Deleting a clip removes its App Storage object, clip row, and view records, but **keeps** its SHA-256 fingerprint claim. **Why:** deletion should not let a later uploader seize an original creator's first-owner right. **How to apply:** never delete the related `content_fingerprints` record as part of content deletion.
- Missing-poster recovery deliberately exhausts its automatic retry allowance rather than retrying forever. **Why:** a valid long video can permanently exceed the derivative processing budget; repeated restarts must not turn it into unlimited background work. **How to apply:** retain bounded processing and durable attempt history; requeue exhausted clips explicitly only after addressing the cause, without changing the no-maximum video-duration policy.
- Do not apply the poster-generation retry cap to deletion cleanup. **Why:** unsupported media can make generation permanently impossible, whereas giving up on deletion after a long storage outage abandons a removal obligation. **How to apply:** back off cleanup attempts without exhausting them; keep media-processing budgets separate.
- Treat orphan reconciliation as evidence, not deletion authorization. **Why:** storage listings and database snapshots are not atomic together, and an expired resumable manifest does not prove that a finalization has stopped. **How to apply:** prefer conservative holds over reclaiming uncertain bytes; require explicit exact-object approval, verified environment ownership, quiesced writers, and fresh reference/generation checks before separate cleanup.
